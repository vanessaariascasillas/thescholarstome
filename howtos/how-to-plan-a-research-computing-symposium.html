How To: Research Computing & Data Symposium

This is our real playbook, cleaned up so it's reusable for next time. Same steps we actually followed, generalized so you can swap in your own venue, dates, and vendors.

Biggest lessons learned, up front: - Start earlier than feels necessary — venue, speakers, catering, and printing all have longer lead times than you'd guess - Set up the shared drive and assign team roles in week one, not later - Keep a separate list for anything invite-only (networking session, leadership dinner) — don't rely on general registration for headcounts - Send dietary restrictions, poster instructions, and reminder emails earlier than you think you need to - Assign day-of stations ahead of time so "all hands on deck" actually means something - Thank-yous, photos, and surveys go out fast (within about a week) — momentum and memory both fade quick

Step 1: Save the Date

  • Send a save-the-date to your mailing list and partner departments as soon as the date is locked in
  • Include: event name, date, host institution, and a placeholder link for "more info to come"

Note: sending this early, even before the venue and speakers are fully confirmed, helps people block their calendars before something else fills the slot.

Step 16: Send the Students' Poster Information

  • Where to turn it in
  • How to turn it in
  • Size of a poster
  • Prizes
  • They print it out themselves
  • Any other info they need:

We will provide the poster board and easels, but you must print your poster. Please print with the following specifications: - 36 in x 48 in (3 ft x 4 ft) - Portrait orientation - Full color

Note: send this early — printing posters is usually the last thing students get to.

Step 17: Send Reminder Emails

  • Registration
  • Posters for students
  • Invites

Note: one reminder about a week out, one about 2–3 days out. Any more and people start tuning them out.

Step 18: Walkthrough and Pivots

  • Walk the empty space
  • Table setup

Note: do this in person if you can — floor plans never quite match reality.

Note: get these uploaded within a day or two — you'll want them for thank-you posts while it's still timely.

Step 23: Student Poster (Digital)

  • Send an email to all poster participants
  • Collect and display the digital posters on the website

Note: this is the easiest step to forget once the event's over — put it on the calendar before you're in the room.

Step 24: Send Surveys

  • Send a post-event survey to attendees, speakers, and volunteers
  • Ask about: overall experience, session quality, logistics/venue, and what to improve for next time
  • Ask if they'd be interested in attending or presenting again next year

Note: send this within a few days while the event's still fresh — response rates drop fast after that.

TL;DR — Biggest Lessons Learned

  • Lead times are longer than you think. Venue, catering, printing, A/V, signage — start these earlier than feels necessary.
  • Set up the drive and assign roles in week one. Retrofitting either mid-planning is a pain.
  • Keep invite-only lists separate from general registration. Networking sessions and leadership dinners need their own headcount.
  • Send things earlier than "on time." Dietary restrictions, poster instructions, and reminder emails all land better with lead time.
  • Assign day-of stations before the day. "All hands on deck" only works if people already know their post.
  • Close the loop fast. Thank-yous, photos, and surveys should go out within about a week — that's when people are still engaged and momentum is easiest to carry into next year.
