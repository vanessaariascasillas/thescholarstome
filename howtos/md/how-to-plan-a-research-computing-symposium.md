# How To: Research Computing & Data Symposium

This is our real playbook, cleaned up so it's reusable for next time. Same steps we actually followed, generalized so you can swap in your own venue, dates, and vendors.

**Biggest lessons learned, up front:**
- Start earlier than feels necessary — venue, speakers, catering, and printing all have longer lead times than you'd guess
- Set up the shared drive and assign team roles in week one, not later
- Keep a separate list for anything invite-only (networking session, leadership dinner) — don't rely on general registration for headcounts
- Send dietary restrictions, poster instructions, and reminder emails earlier than you think you need to
- Assign day-of stations ahead of time so "all hands on deck" actually means something
- Thank-yous, photos, and surveys go out fast (within about a week) — momentum and memory both fade quick

## Step 24: Send Surveys

- Send a post-event survey to attendees, speakers, and volunteers
- Ask about: overall experience, session quality, logistics/venue, and what to improve for next time
- Ask if they'd be interested in attending or presenting again next year

Note: send this within a few days while the event's still fresh — response rates drop fast after that.

## TL;DR — Biggest Lessons Learned

- **Lead times are longer than you think.** Venue, catering, printing, A/V, signage — start these earlier than feels necessary.
- **Set up the drive and assign roles in week one.** Retrofitting either mid-planning is a pain.
- **Keep invite-only lists separate from general registration.** Networking sessions and leadership dinners need their own headcount.
- **Send things earlier than "on time."** Dietary restrictions, poster instructions, and reminder emails all land better with lead time.
- **Assign day-of stations before the day.** "All hands on deck" only works if people already know their post.
- **Close the loop fast.** Thank-yous, photos, and surveys should go out within about a week — that's when people are still engaged and momentum is easiest to carry into next year.
